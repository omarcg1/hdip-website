Health, education and social and economic justice — the three areas HDIP focused on in 2023.

Programme directions

During 2023, HDIP focused on three main areas: health issues, education, and social, economic and development justice. These areas are particularly important because of their role in achieving genuine community development and building a society in which individuals' basic needs are met.

HDIP placed particular emphasis on youth and women, recognising that these groups are among those most in need of support and assistance, and that reaching them extends the programme's services to the widest possible segment of society.

Health for All in 2023

01

The right to health

The principle that underpins and is promoted throughout all HDIP programmes, as a fundamental human right. Every individual should have access to the healthcare services necessary to achieve and maintain good health.

02

Reducing health gaps

Raising awareness of "Health for All" to reduce disparities arising from economic, social or geographical factors, ensuring services reach marginalised groups.

03

Prevention and awareness

Training that emphasises prevention and health education; when individuals receive accurate information they become better able to make informed decisions.

04

Community participation

Encouraging local community organisations to participate in health-related decision-making, strengthening programme effectiveness and health policy influence.

05

Improving quality of life

Ensuring access to healthcare to improve quality of life and life expectancy, through inclusion, prevention and community participation.

Activities delivered

  • Health awareness workshops222 workshops reaching approximately 2,000 participants across the West Bank and Gaza Strip, covering reproductive health, school health, personal hygiene, first aid, healthy nutrition, environmental health, psychological first aid and child health.
  • Training in health education skills200 participants trained to deliver workshops in their own communities, with particular emphasis on marginalised and remote areas far from service centres.
  • Awareness and advocacy campaignsCampaigns in marginalised areas advocating for the provision of healthcare and primary healthcare services.
  • Specific health trainingsReproductive health (Bethlehem); smoking cessation (Jenin); stress management (Ramallah); child health (Hebron, Jenin, Tulkarm); three environmental health courses; healthy habits for adults; emergency first aid across eight areas; crisis crowd management; psychological first aid (Jenin, Tulkarm); first aid for children in war situations.
  • Women Capable of Changing SocietyImplemented in Hebron, Jenin and Tulkarm, with 23 trainees per governorate across 20 villages or towns each. Training covered personal and life skills, social media marketing for handicrafts, income-generating small businesses, access to finance, local election awareness, personal financial planning, ICT and women's mental health.
  • Women Leaders in Local Councils62 training hours covering women's political empowerment, women's role in the electoral process, local election campaign management and effective participation, plus two exchange visits between project locations which produced a working paper on key strategies.
  • Youth Leadership Development in Universities62 hours of advanced capacity building covering social media, political parties, participation, campaigns, gender, advocacy and lobbying, non-violence, leadership, democracy, human rights and networking.
  • Student in Need Fund campaignAn awareness campaign launched at seven local universities — Al-Quds Open, An-Najah National, Birzeit, Hebron, Al-Ahliya, Arab American and Al-Quds University–Abu Dis — using posters, brochures, workshops, discussion sessions and social media, with materials developed by the beneficiary students themselves.
  • Right to EducationAn electronic survey found 99% of participants considered education important; 31% because it helps them obtain suitable employment and 20% because it strengthens their leadership abilities. Awareness workshops, science and life-skills courses, and three career guidance meetings in Bethlehem, Nablus and Gaza followed.
