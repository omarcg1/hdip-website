About HDIP

Founded in 1989 by researchers, health practitioners and development experts, in response to the urgent need to improve health care in Palestine — particularly in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip — through a scientific and professional approach to research, planning and development policy.

Al-Bireh, Ramallah governorate — أمين · CC BY-SA 4.0 · Photography & credits

Who we are

HDIP is an independent, non-profit Palestinian organisation. It dedicates its work to specialised research, consultancy and the development of public policy across development sectors.

Over time its role expanded to advocating for the rights of marginalised groups in Palestinian society — women, youth and persons with disabilities — rooted in a conviction about the importance of social justice and equality of opportunity.

The institute gives considerable attention to building a democratic Palestinian civil society founded on active participation, transparency and accountability. It provides accurate information, evaluation studies and specialised training programmes in health, systems management and development policy, in order to strengthen local institutions and improve the quality of services delivered to citizens.

Our vision

We seek to advance development in Palestine by enabling Palestinian society to reach its basic needs in health, education and social services, fairly and sustainably. We believe a strong and cohesive society begins with meeting these vital needs and with development policies that respond to citizens' aspirations and reflect their real priorities. We work in a spirit of responsibility and community partnership to support positive change and to establish the principles of social justice and good governance — towards a prosperous Palestinian future that guarantees dignity and opportunity for all.

Our mission

HDIP seeks to contribute actively to building a Palestinian society founded on social and economic justice, which upholds the values of freedom, democracy and human rights. The programme works by implementing activities and initiatives that strengthen Palestinian capacities, open horizons for constructive dialogue and meet urgent community needs. We believe purposeful media is a tool for change, a platform for the citizen's voice, and a bridge towards a more aware and solidary society. Through it we seek to establish a culture of community participation, to stimulate critical thinking, and to support policies that place people at the heart of development.

Our objectives

The fifteen general objectives set out in HDIP's founding profile.

  1. 01Conduct research and studies on health and development conditions and on health and development policies.
  2. 02Hold seminars, meetings and conferences, and issue publications, research, books, periodicals and booklets in its field.
  3. 03Run programmes and training courses, and provide research, training, media and technical services in health and development, administration, administrative development and development planning.
  4. 04Raise the level of health and development awareness, and establish health information and research databases.
  5. 05Conduct dialogues and coordination activities to improve cooperation between civil society organisations and the relevant ministries, in order to improve coordination and rationalise the use of development resources.
  6. 06Provide advice and organise the scientific and technical activities that assist in preparing and adopting appropriate health, social and development legislation.
  7. 07Prepare, publish and document research abstracts in a way that serves Palestinian researchers, academics and development policy makers.
  8. 08Provide the consultations, evaluations, studies and expertise needed to strengthen individuals and institutions.
  9. 09Publish information that contributes to developing health and development awareness and improving development capacities, and provide the advisory, research and technical services that support sound development planning.
  10. 10Cooperate with Arab and international institutions in health and development research and statistics, and in developing health and development policies.
  11. 11Organise health and development programmes and activities.
  12. 12Supply and assist students, researchers, planners and local councils with information.
  13. 13Issue maps, explanatory materials, publications, books and periodicals that follow health issues and general development policies.
  14. 14Develop cooperation and coordination in the health and development fields between civil society and governmental institutions, in a way that serves Palestinian society.
  15. 15Carry out media activities that support the cause of the Palestinian people and meet its needs.

What we do

HDIP works across seven interconnected areas. Five of them are presented on this site as programme areas with their own pages.

  • Health and Health EducationPromoting health awareness, prevention, healthy behaviours, mental health, and access to reliable health information.
  • Education and Youth DevelopmentSupporting students through educational programmes, training, mentoring, community libraries, skills development and access to learning opportunities.
  • Women's EmpowermentDeveloping programmes that strengthen women's knowledge, skills, participation, resilience and access to economic and social opportunities.
  • Community DevelopmentWorking with local communities and institutions to identify needs and develop practical, sustainable responses.
  • Research, Policy and AdvocacyConducting research, assessments and policy-oriented activities that generate evidence and support informed decision-making.
  • Capacity BuildingProviding training and technical support to individuals, civil society organisations and local institutions.
  • Monitoring, Evaluation and LearningApplying systematic approaches to monitoring implementation, measuring results, documenting lessons and improving programme quality.

Our approach

HDIP places people and communities at the centre of development. Our programmes are designed based on identified needs and implemented through participatory approaches that encourage local ownership and meaningful engagement.

We seek to connect research with practical action, ensuring that programme design is informed by evidence while remaining responsive to the realities faced by communities. We also promote partnerships and coordination among civil society organisations, educational institutions, governmental bodies and other stakeholders.

Why partner with us

HDIP is committed to implementing donor-supported programmes with professionalism, transparency, accountability and effective resource management. We value long-term partnerships and work closely with donors and implementing partners to ensure that agreed objectives, activities, outputs and reporting requirements are met.

  1. Clear and measurable programme objectives and results.
  2. Transparent financial and administrative procedures.
  3. Regular monitoring, documentation and reporting.
  4. Evidence-based programme design and decision-making.
  5. Community participation and stakeholder engagement.
  6. Continuous learning and improvement.
  7. Sustainability and institutional capacity development.

With more than three decades of experience, HDIP offers partners a combination of local knowledge, technical expertise, community access, programme implementation capacity and commitment to accountability. Through collaboration with donors and development partners, HDIP seeks to translate resources and knowledge into practical, measurable and sustainable benefits for Palestinian communities.
